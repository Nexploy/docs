---
title: Organizations
description: Group your repositories and collaborate with a team on a single instance
---

An **organization** groups repositories together with the members allowed to access them. Every repository belongs to an organization, and that organization determines who can see it and act on it.

<Callout title="Why it matters">
  Without organizations, any user with the right role could act on **every**
  resource in the instance. Organization membership is now checked for each
  action on a repository, a build, a container or a domain.
</Callout>

## Two permission levels

Nexploy combines two role systems that should not be confused:

| Level | Roles | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Instance** | `guest`, `developer`, `admin` | Global administration: users, Traefik, registries, SSL certificates, backups, settings |
| **Organization** | `owner`, `admin`, `member` | Resources of one specific organization: repositories, builds, pipelines, environment variables, domains, containers |

Acting on a resource requires **both**: the instance role allows the type of action, the organization role grants access to that particular resource.

A user with the `admin` instance role can reach every organization, so support operations remain possible.

## Organization roles

- **`member`** — browses repositories, starts builds, manages the organization's containers. Cannot create or delete repositories, nor edit environment variables.
- **`admin`** — manages repositories, pipelines, environment variables, domains and deployments. Invites and removes members, and can rename the organization.
- **`owner`** — everything an `admin` can do, plus changing member roles and deleting the organization.

<Callout type="warn" title="The owner role cannot be handed over">
  The only assignable roles are `member` and `admin`, both on invitation and on a
  role change. The `owner` is the account that created the organization, and
  stays that way — there is no ownership transfer in the application.
</Callout>

## Inviting a member

Invitations happen **in-app**, with no email involved.

1. In the sidebar's organization switcher, open the organization's **⋯** menu, then **Members**.
2. Click **Invite member** and enter the email address.
3. Pick the role (`member` or `admin`).

The invited person sees the invitation on their **Account** page and accepts or declines it in one click.

<Callout type="warn" title="The account must already exist">
  Because open sign-up is disabled in Nexploy, you can only invite an address
  that matches an existing account. Create the user first from
  **Administration → Users**.
</Callout>

Invitations expire after 7 days. An organization accepts up to 100 members.

## Switching organization

The switcher in the sidebar, next to the logo, moves between organizations. It is also where everything is managed: each organization's **⋯** submenu gives access to members, renaming and deletion, and the entry at the bottom of the list creates one.

The active organization determines two things:

- the **repositories you see** — only those of the active organization;
- the organization **new repositories are attached to**.

## Creating an organization

From the sidebar switcher, **Create organization**. The `developer` or `admin` instance role is required; a `guest` cannot create one.

The creator automatically becomes its `owner`.

## Leaving an organization

You don't need an admin to get out of an organization you were invited to. In the sidebar switcher, open the organization's **⋯** menu and pick **Leave organization**.

Leaving revokes your access immediately: its repositories, builds and containers disappear from your view. Nothing is deleted — the organization and its resources stay untouched for the remaining members. Coming back requires a new invitation.

If the organization you left was the active one, Nexploy switches you to another of your organizations automatically.

<Callout type="warn" title="A sole owner cannot leave">
  The option is hidden for an organization where you are the only `owner` — that
  would leave it without one, and ownership cannot be handed over. Delete the
  organization instead (after moving its repositories elsewhere). Your personal
  organization, however, can be neither left nor deleted: it is your permanent
  fallback.
</Callout>

## Moving a repository to another organization

A repository isn't stuck in the organization it was created in: from the repository's **Settings** tab, the move section transfers it to another organization.

The transfer requires rights on **both sides**: permission to update the repository in its current organization, and permission to create repositories in the target one — in practice, being `admin` or `owner` on both (an instance `admin` bypasses this).

<Callout type="warn" title="The Git account doesn't follow the members">
  The repository keeps the Git account it was connected with, the one belonging to
  the user who created it. After a transfer, members of the target organization
  can access the repository but clones still use those credentials; if that
  account is disconnected, the repository has to be relinked to another Git account.
</Callout>

## Safeguards

- The **last `owner`** of an organization cannot be removed, demoted, or leave it.
- An organization that **still owns repositories** cannot be deleted. Move them to another organization or delete them beforehand.
- The **personal organization** cannot be renamed, invited into, left or deleted — see below.

## Personal organization

Every user automatically gets a personal organization when their account is created, named after them and owned by them. That is also what happened when upgrading from an earlier version: every existing user received theirs, with their repositories attached, with no manual action and nothing lost.

It is deliberately **single-occupant and immutable**: it is your own workspace, not a team you administer. Four operations are therefore blocked on it, at the server level and not only in the interface:

| Blocked | Why |
|---|---|
| **Renaming** | Its identity is tied to the account it belongs to |
| **Inviting a member** | It stays yours alone; collaborate through a regular organization |
| **Members page** | It would hold a single row, yours, with no action available |
| **Leaving** | You are its only `owner`, and ownership cannot be handed over |
| **Deleting** | It is the fallback your repositories and your session land on |

An instance `admin` doesn't bypass these: the restriction applies to anyone's personal organization, including from an admin account.

Since none of these actions apply, the **⋯** menu doesn't appear at all next to the personal organization in the switcher — it can be selected, and that's it. The URL of its members page returns a 404. To work with other people, create a regular organization and move the repositories into it.

<Callout type="warn" title="Deleting a user deletes their personal organization">
  And deleting an organization cascades to its repositories. This path does not go
  through the safeguard above: before deleting an account from
  **Administration → Users**, move any repository still sitting in their personal
  organization.
</Callout>
